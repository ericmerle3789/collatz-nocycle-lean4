\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

This section fixes notation and defines the formal predicate
\texttt{IsOddCycle} used throughout the paper. All definitions here are
natural-language renderings of Lean objects in
\texttt{ProjetCollatz/Phase50CycleEquation.lean} and adjacent files; the
Lean source is authoritative.

\subsection{The Collatz map and its Syracuse acceleration}

We write \(\collatz : \Nat \to \Nat\) for the Collatz map
(Section~\ref{sec:introduction}). For the purposes of cycle exclusion it
is convenient to accelerate past the even steps. Define, for an odd
integer \(n\), the \emph{Syracuse successor} \(S(n)\) as the unique odd
integer obtained from \(n\) by applying \(\collatz\) once and then
repeatedly dividing by \(2\) until the result is odd. Formally :
\[
S(n) = \frac{3n + 1}{2^{\nu_2(3n+1)}},
\]
where \(\nu_2\) denotes the \(2\)-adic valuation. The Syracuse map \(S\)
is well defined on positive odd integers.

\subsection{Odd cycles}

An \emph{odd cycle} of length \(k\) is a sequence of \(k\) positive odd
integers \(n_0, n_1, \ldots, n_{k-1}\) with
\(S(n_i) = n_{(i+1) \bmod k}\) for all \(i\). The loop
\(1 \mapsto 1\) (length \(k = 1\)) is the \emph{trivial} odd cycle.
A \emph{nontrivial} odd cycle is one of length \(k \geq 2\) containing a
value other than \(1\).

\subsection{The \texttt{IsOddCycle} predicate}

The repository's central predicate is
\(\mathtt{IsOddCycle} : \Nat \to \Nat \to \mathtt{Prop}\). Intuitively,
\texttt{IsOddCycle n k} asserts that \(n\) is a positive odd integer
strictly greater than \(1\), and that the Syracuse orbit of length
\(k\) starting at \(n\) returns to \(n\). The formal definition does
not require \(n\) to be minimal along the orbit; any element of a
cycle satisfies the predicate (for its own length \(k\) and possibly
a rotated starting index). The theorems proved in the repository's
Phase~50 modules establish the equivalence of this predicate with
several classical formulations, including the \emph{Steiner equation}
\(n_0 \cdot d = \text{corrSum}(A)\) where \(A\) is the sequence of
\(2\)-adic valuations along the orbit and \(d = 2^S - 3^k\) with
\(S = \sum_i A_i\)~\cite{Steiner1977}.

In the formal statement of our main theorem, \texttt{IsOddCycle n k} plays
the role of the hypothesis to be refuted : we conclude
\texttt{False} from it, conditional on the three structural hypotheses of
Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}.

\subsection{Baker's theorem and irrationality measures}

We briefly record the form of Baker's theorem relevant to our formulation.
Baker's theorem~\cite{Baker1966} on linear forms in the logarithms of
algebraic numbers implies an effective lower bound :
there exist computable constants \(C_0, \mu\) such that, for all positive
integers \(s, k\) with \(2^s > 3^k\),
\[
|s \log 2 - k \log 3| \geq C_0 \cdot k^{-\mu}.
\]
An equivalent reformulation, after multiplying through and using
\(2^s - 3^k > 0\), gives an inequality of the form
\((2^s - 3^k) \cdot k^{\mu} \geq C_1 \cdot 3^k\) for some constant
\(C_1\) and sufficiently large \(k\). The Rhin refinement~\cite{Rhin1987}
reduces \(\mu\) to at most \(5.125\) for the pair \((\log 2, \log 3)\). In
the Lean statement of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:baker}, we take \(\mu = 6\), a
deliberate weakening that trades finer-grained constants for a cleaner
integer statement (see Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}).

\subsection{Continued fractions of \(\log_2 3\)}

The continued-fraction expansion of \(\log_2 3\) produces a sequence of
convergents \(p_i / q_i\) minimising
\(|q_i \log_2 3 - p_i|\) among rationals with denominator at most \(q_i\)
(Legendre's best-approximation theorem, see
\cite{HardyWright1979,Khinchin1964}). The convergent denominators
\(q_i\) grow rapidly; the argument behind
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:derived} uses a windowing of these convergents to
derive the \(k > 1322\) case of the main theorem (see
Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} for a sketch and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} for the planned full formalisation).
