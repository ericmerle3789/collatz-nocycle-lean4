\section{Lean Formalisation}
\label{sec:lean}

This section describes the Lean~4 formalisation of the main result and the
choices made to preserve its auditability. All claims are reproducible
by cloning the repository and running \texttt{./reproduce.sh}
(see Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}). The overall approach --- a
conditional proof with an explicit axiom baseline and a public
reproducibility pipeline --- is in the tradition of formal-proof-of-
conjecture papers such as Gonthier's Four Colour Theorem
formalisation~\cite{Gonthier2008} and the Flyspeck
project~\cite{Hales2017Flyspeck}.

\subsection{Toolchain and dependencies}

The formalisation uses \textbf{Lean~4, toolchain \texttt{leanprover/lean4:v4.27.0}},
pinned in the repository file \texttt{lean-toolchain}. The dependency on
\emph{Mathlib} is pinned to revision \texttt{v4.27.0} in \texttt{lakefile.toml}
(compatible with the Lean toolchain). Upgrading either component is a
classified~P1 action requiring an ADR entry (see
\texttt{docs/BIBLE/DECISIONS.md}) and passes of the pre-commit verification
protocol.

\subsection{Repository structure}

The central result is stated and proved in the module
\texttt{ProjetCollatz.Phase59ContinuedFractions}. The broader library
\texttt{ProjetCollatz} consists of 36 \texttt{.lean} files organised in
development phases (\texttt{Phase6} through \texttt{Phase59}), totalling 393
theorems and lemmas. The top-level file \texttt{ProjetCollatz.lean} imports
the modules required for the main theorem.

\subsection{The central theorem}

The statement of the main theorem, as formalised, is :

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Central theorem declaration (\texttt{Phase59ContinuedFractions.lean}:236).},label={lst:central}]
theorem no_nontrivial_cycle_phase59
    (baker : BakerSeparation) (barina : BarinaVerification)
    (cf : DerivedLargeKBound)
    (n k : N) (hcyc : IsOddCycle n k) : False
\end{lstlisting}

The theorem takes the three external hypotheses as explicit
parameters (see Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}) and concludes
\texttt{False} under the assumption that \(n\) and \(k\) define a nontrivial
odd cycle. The proof is a case split on \(k \leq 1322\) versus \(k > 1322\),
closed by distinct sub-lemmas (\texttt{no\_cycle\_k\_le\_1322} and
\texttt{no\_cycle\_k\_gt\_1322}). All intermediate lemmas of the central
chain are fully proved in Lean with no \texttt{sorry}.

\subsection{Structures instead of axioms}

For each of the three external hypotheses (see Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}),
we deliberately use a Lean \texttt{structure} rather than a user-declared
\texttt{axiom} :

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={\texttt{DerivedLargeKBound} as a structure (\texttt{Phase59ContinuedFractions.lean}:115).}]
structure DerivedLargeKBound where
  large_k_bound : \forall (n k : N),
    IsOddCycle n k \rightarrow k > 1322 \rightarrow n < 2 ^ 71
\end{lstlisting}

This choice has two consequences :

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A Lean \texttt{structure} field is \emph{not} an axiom of the
        ambient theory. Its content must be provided by whoever
        instantiates the structure, and the proof term that does so is
        itself subject to axiom auditing. Replacing the \texttt{structure}
        with a proven theorem is therefore a strictly additive refinement
        that does not require changing the statement of the main theorem.
  \item The \texttt{\#print axioms} probe on the main theorem reports only
        those axioms that its proof actually uses, \emph{not} those that
        would be needed to prove the structural content of the hypothesis.
        This is the basis of the axiom discipline recorded in
        \texttt{expected\_axioms.md}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Axiom baseline}
\label{sec:axioms}

The \texttt{\#print axioms} probe on the main theorem yields :

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
'ProjetCollatz.no_nontrivial_cycle_phase59' depends on axioms:
  [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]
\end{lstlisting}

These are the three standard kernel axioms of Mathlib, required by
essentially every non-constructive theorem that uses classical reasoning
and quotient types. No user-declared \texttt{axiom} appears in
\texttt{ProjetCollatz}. The same three axioms and no others appear for the
variant theorems \texttt{no\_nontrivial\_cycle\_\{final,derived,full\}} and
the immediate sub-lemmas \texttt{no\_cycle\_k\_le\_1322},
\texttt{no\_cycle\_k\_gt\_1322}, and \texttt{sdw\_from\_cf}.

The file \texttt{expected\_axioms.md} is the canonical baseline against which
all commits are checked. Its contents are enforced by
\texttt{probes/check\_central\_axioms.lean} invoked from
\texttt{reproduce.sh}, which fails with exit code~3 on any axiom drift.

\subsection{Auxiliary lemmas using \texttt{native\_decide}}

Certain computational facts used to justify the content of
\texttt{DerivedLargeKBound} at the mathematical level are, in the repository,
stated as standalone auxiliary lemmas. Examples include arithmetic
separations such as :

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Representative \texttt{native\_decide} lemma (\texttt{Phase59ContinuedFractions.lean}:128).}]
theorem cf_gap_8 : 2 * (2 : N) ^ 1055 \geq 3 * (3 : N) ^ 665 := by native_decide
\end{lstlisting}

These lemmas depend on the tactic \texttt{native\_decide}, which in Lean~4
introduces the axioms \texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool} and
\texttt{Lean.trustCompiler}. The \texttt{\#print axioms} probe confirms
this :

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
'ProjetCollatz.cf_gap_8' depends on axioms:
  [propext, Lean.ofReduceBool, Lean.trustCompiler]
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{remark}[Isolation of \texttt{native\_decide} from the central chain]
At the current stage, \texttt{cf\_gap\_\*} and related
\texttt{cf\_nbound\_\*} lemmas are \emph{not} transitively used by the main
theorem, because \texttt{DerivedLargeKBound} is taken as an hypothesis
(a \texttt{structure} parameter). Consequently,
\texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool} and \texttt{Lean.trustCompiler} do not appear in
the axiom list of \texttt{no\_nontrivial\_cycle\_phase59}. Should future
work (see Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}) promote
\texttt{DerivedLargeKBound} from a structure to a proven theorem using
these lemmas, these two axioms would enter the central chain. We plan to
declare them explicitly in \texttt{expected\_axioms.md} at that point and
to acknowledge them in a subsequent version of this paper.
\end{remark}
