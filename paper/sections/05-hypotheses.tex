\section{Hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

The main theorem depends on three structural hypotheses, each formalised
as a Lean \texttt{structure} and supplied to the theorem as an explicit
parameter. Two of the three correspond to published results; the third is
derived from a continued-fractions argument and is stated as a conjecture
to be formalised in future work. This section describes each hypothesis
in turn.

\subsection{\textsf{BakerSeparation} (B1)}

\begin{hypothesis}[Baker separation, formalised]\label{hyp:baker}
There exists a structural witness \textsf{BakerSeparation} providing the
Lean function
\[
\forall\, s, k \in \Nat,\ s \geq 1 \wedge k \geq 2 \wedge 2^s > 3^k \;\Longrightarrow\;
(2^s - 3^k) \cdot k^6 \geq 3^k .
\]
\end{hypothesis}

% [ERIC-REVIEW] The constant exponent 6 in k^6 is a deliberate weakening
% of the Rhin bound. Please verify the following explanation is accurate.

The inequality corresponds to a lower bound on \(|s \log 2 - k \log 3|\)
of the form \(|s \log 2 - k \log 3| \gtrsim k^{-\mu}\), where
\(\mu\) is an irrationality-measure-type exponent for \(\log_2 3\). Taking
\(\mu = 6\) is strictly weaker than Rhin's finer bound
\(\mu(\log_2 3) \leq 5.125\)~\cite{Rhin1987}; we use \(\mu = 6\) in the
Lean formulation because it admits a clean integer statement and matches
the subsequent arithmetic at the price of a slightly larger threshold for
the case split (see \textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} below).

Hypothesis~\textsf{BakerSeparation} is a direct consequence of Baker's
theorem on linear forms in logarithms of algebraic
numbers~\cite{Baker1966}, strengthened by subsequent work on effective
irrationality measures. We supply it as a structural hypothesis in the
Lean formalisation to keep the main theorem conditional on a clearly
identified published result, rather than as a user-declared axiom.

\subsection{\textsf{BarinaVerification} (B2)}

\begin{hypothesis}[Barina computational verification, formalised]\label{hyp:barina}
There exists a structural witness \textsf{BarinaVerification} providing
the Lean function
\[
\forall\, n \in \Nat,\ n > 0 \wedge n < 2^{71} \;\Longrightarrow\;
\text{\texttt{reaches\_one}}(n).
\]
\end{hypothesis}

This hypothesis corresponds to Barina's published computational
verification~\cite{Barina2025} that every positive integer below
\(2^{71}\) reaches \(1\) under the Collatz iteration. Since a Collatz
cycle containing a value below \(2^{71}\) would contradict this
verification, \textsf{BarinaVerification} is equivalent, for our
purposes, to a small-\(n\) exclusion.

The DOI \texttt{10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0} has been resolved as of
2026-04-22 (see \texttt{docs/BIBLE/env-snapshots/2026-04-22-doi-check.txt}
in the repository).

\subsection{\textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} (B3)}

\begin{hypothesis}[Derived large-\(k\) bound]\label{hyp:derived}
There exists a structural witness \textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} providing
the Lean function
\[
\forall\, n, k \in \Nat,\ \text{\texttt{IsOddCycle}}\,n\,k \wedge k > 1322
\;\Longrightarrow\; n < 2^{71}.
\]
\end{hypothesis}

Unlike the first two, \textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} is not, to our
knowledge, a result published as a standalone theorem. The mathematical
justification for its content rests on two ingredients :

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The continued-fraction expansion of \(\log_2 3\), whose
        convergents \(p_i / q_i\) approximate \(\log_2 3\) optimally
        among rationals with denominator at most \(q_i\) (Legendre's
        best-approximation theorem, see for example
        \cite{HardyWright1979,Khinchin1964}). The sequence of denominators
        \(q_i\) is well studied; the ones relevant here are bracketed by
        the threshold \(k = 1322\) in the formal statement.
  \item The Baker-type inequality of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:baker} combined
        with a product-bound argument on the sequence of odd iterates in
        a hypothetical cycle. Given a putative cycle of length \(k\)
        with seed \(n\), one obtains an inequality of the form
        \(n \lesssim F(k)\) where \(F\) is explicit in terms of
        \((2^s - 3^k)\)-type quantities.
\end{enumerate}

Combining the two, one can derive a bound \(n < 2^{71}\) for all cycle
parameters \(k\) above a computable threshold. The specific threshold
\(1322\) reflects the choice \(\mu = 6\) in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:baker} and
the precise windows of continued-fraction convergents that the argument
uses; a finer \(\mu\) (e.g.\ Rhin's \(5.125\)) would permit a lower
threshold. A full mathematical derivation of the specific value \(1322\)
in the repository's proof supporting modules is outside the scope of this
paper and is the target of the future work sketched in
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\begin{remark}[Why a \texttt{structure}, not an \texttt{axiom}]
We emphasise that, in the Lean formalisation,
\textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} is a \emph{structure}, not a user-declared
axiom. Consequently, promoting it to a proven Lean theorem (the target of
Phase Legendre; see Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}) would be strictly
additive : the statement of the main theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) would not change, only its axiom list would be
updated to reflect the additional machinery used in the new proof of the
former structure (specifically, the \texttt{native\_decide} axioms
\texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool} and \texttt{Lean.trustCompiler}, as discussed
in Section~\ref{sec:axioms}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Integrity of the conditional form}

We consider the conditional form \emph{Baker + Barina + DerivedLargeKBound
\(\Rightarrow\) no nontrivial odd cycle} to be a meaningful intermediate
step, for three reasons :

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Both \textsf{BakerSeparation} and \textsf{BarinaVerification}
        are backed by published, peer-reviewed work.
  \item \textsf{DerivedLargeKBound}, while not independently published,
        is a \emph{derivation} from published ingredients; its content
        is not circularly tied to the conclusion of the main theorem.
  \item The partition \(k \leq 1322\) (Barina-driven) versus \(k > 1322\)
        (CF-derived) is decided by a named threshold, making the
        boundary between published and derived ingredients explicit in
        the code rather than buried in a proof.
\end{enumerate}
