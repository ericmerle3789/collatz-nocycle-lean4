\section{Hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

The main theorem depends on three structural hypotheses, each formalised
as a Lean \texttt{structure} and supplied to the theorem as an explicit
parameter. Two of the three correspond to published results; the third is
derived from a continued-fractions argument and is stated as a conjecture
to be formalised in future work. This section describes each hypothesis
in turn. The restriction to odd cycles (\texttt{IsOddCycle}) is without
loss of generality for the cycle problem ; see Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}.

\subsection{\textsf{BakerSeparation} (B1)}

\begin{hypothesis}[Baker separation, formalised]\label{hyp:baker}
There exists a structural witness \textsf{BakerSeparation} providing the
Lean function
\[
\forall\, s, k \in \Nat,\ s \geq 1 \wedge k \geq 2 \wedge 2^s > 3^k \;\Longrightarrow\;
(2^s - 3^k) \cdot k^6 \geq 3^k .
\]
\end{hypothesis}

The inequality corresponds to a lower bound on \(|s \log 2 - k \log 3|\)
of the form \(|s \log 2 - k \log 3| \gtrsim k^{-\mu}\), where
\(\mu\) is the effective irrationality exponent for \(\log_2 3\) in the
sense of Baker~\cite{Baker1966}, refined by Rhin~\cite{Rhin1987} to
\(\mu(\log_2 3) \leq 5.125\). In the Lean formulation we adopt
\(\mu = 6\), a strict weakening of Rhin's bound, for formalisation
reasons only : an integer exponent produces cleaner arithmetic in the
supporting Lean modules and avoids manipulating non-integer constants
at the kernel level. The \emph{cost} of this choice is a larger
threshold in the case split of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} (the value
\(1322\) discussed in \textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} below). Using Rhin's
\(\mu = 5.125\) directly would yield a smaller threshold ; optimising
this is left to future work (see Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

Hypothesis~\textsf{BakerSeparation} is a direct consequence of Baker's
theorem on linear forms in logarithms of algebraic
numbers~\cite{Baker1966}, strengthened by subsequent work on effective
irrationality measures. We supply it as a structural hypothesis in the
Lean formalisation to keep the main theorem conditional on an
explicitly identified published result, rather than as a user-declared
axiom.

\subsection{\textsf{BarinaVerification} (B2)}

\begin{hypothesis}[Barina computational verification, formalised]\label{hyp:barina}
There exists a structural witness \textsf{BarinaVerification} providing
the Lean function
\[
\forall\, n \in \Nat,\ n > 0 \wedge n < 2^{71} \;\Longrightarrow\;
\text{\texttt{reaches\_one}}(n).
\]
\end{hypothesis}

This hypothesis corresponds to Barina's published computational
verification~\cite{Barina2025} that every positive integer below
\(2^{71}\) reaches \(1\) under the Collatz iteration. Since a Collatz
cycle containing a value below \(2^{71}\) would contradict this
verification, \textsf{BarinaVerification} is equivalent, for our
purposes, to a small-\(n\) exclusion.


\subsection{\textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} (B3)}

\begin{hypothesis}[Derived large-\(k\) bound]\label{hyp:derived}
There exists a structural witness \textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} providing
the Lean function
\[
\forall\, n, k \in \Nat,\ \text{\texttt{IsOddCycle}}\,n\,k \wedge k > 1322
\;\Longrightarrow\; n < 2^{71}.
\]
\end{hypothesis}

Unlike the first two, \textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} is not, to our
knowledge, a result published as a standalone theorem. The mathematical
justification for its content rests on two ingredients :

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The continued-fraction expansion of \(\log_2 3\), whose
        convergents \(p_i / q_i\) approximate \(\log_2 3\) optimally
        among rationals with denominator at most \(q_i\) (Legendre's
        best-approximation theorem, see for example
        \cite{HardyWright1979,Khinchin1964}). The sequence of denominators
        \(q_i\) is well studied; the ones relevant here are bracketed by
        the threshold \(k = 1322\) in the formal statement.
  \item The Baker-type inequality of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:baker} combined
        with a product-bound argument on the sequence of odd iterates in
        a hypothetical cycle. Given a putative cycle of length \(k\)
        with seed \(n\), one obtains an inequality of the form
        \(n \lesssim F(k)\) where \(F\) is explicit in terms of
        \((2^s - 3^k)\)-type quantities.
\end{enumerate}

Combining the two, one can derive a bound \(n < 2^{71}\) for all cycle
parameters \(k\) above a computable threshold. The mechanism can be
sketched as follows. Substituting Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:baker} with
\(\mu = 6\) into the Product Bound on the sequence of odd iterates of a
hypothetical cycle yields an upper bound of the form
\(n \leq \exp(c \cdot k^\mu \log k)\) for an explicit constant \(c\)
depending on the continued-fraction windows of \(\log_2 3\). Intersecting
this with the requirement \(n \geq 2^{71}\) that would be forced by any
cycle evading Barina's verification
(Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:barina}) yields
\(k \leq K_0\) for some explicit \(K_0\) depending on the CF convergents
(roughly, the \(q_i\) in the window \(q_8, \ldots, q_{13}\) of
\(\log_2 3\)). The paper takes \(K_0 = 1322\) as a safe bound ; the
value is \emph{not claimed to be tight}, and a finer \(\mu\)
(e.g.\ Rhin's \(5.125\) instead of \(6\)) would permit a smaller threshold
(see Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:baker} and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}). A
complete quantitative derivation of \(1322\) from the convergents and the
explicit constant \(c\) lives in the repository's Phase~58--59 modules
and the supporting commentary ; a formal Lean proof (as opposed to the
\texttt{structure} used here) is the target of Phase Legendre (see
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\begin{remark}[Why a \texttt{structure}, not an \texttt{axiom}]
We emphasise that, in the Lean formalisation,
\textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} is a \emph{structure}, not a user-declared
axiom. Consequently, promoting it to a proven Lean theorem (the target of
Phase Legendre; see Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}) would be strictly
additive \emph{at the statement level}: the statement of the main theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) would not change. At the level of the trust
base, however, the axiom list does change : the \texttt{native\_decide}
tactic used by the supporting arithmetic lemmas introduces
\texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool} and \texttt{Lean.trustCompiler} into the chain
of \(\mathtt{no\_nontrivial\_cycle\_phase59}\) once
\textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} is replaced by a proven theorem
(see Section~\ref{sec:axioms}). The headline "three fundamental axioms
only" reported in this paper is thus tied to the current stage of the
formalisation where \textsf{DerivedLargeKBound} remains a \texttt{structure}
parameter. We flag this explicitly rather than leave the escalation
implicit.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scope of the formal theorem}

We state plainly what the Lean theorem proves and what it does not.

\paragraph{What the formal theorem proves.}
The Lean theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) establishes in particular
the branch
\[
\mathsf{B1} \wedge \mathsf{B2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\neg\,\exists\,(n, k)\ \text{with}\ k \leq 1322\ \text{and}\ \texttt{IsOddCycle}\,n\,k,
\]
that is, the non-existence of a nontrivial odd cycle in the range
\(2 \leq k \leq 1322\) under the two published hypotheses
\(\mathsf{B1}\) (Baker separation with \(\mu = 6\)) and
\(\mathsf{B2}\) (Barina's verification to \(2^{71}\)). This is the
sub-lemma \texttt{no\_cycle\_k\_le\_1322} in the proof file. Its proof
is fully formalised in Lean, with the axiom baseline reported in
Section~\ref{sec:axioms}. This sub-lemma does not rely on \(\mathsf{B3}\).

\paragraph{What the formal theorem does not prove.}
For \(k > 1322\), the theorem relies on the structural hypothesis
\(\mathsf{B3}\) (\textsf{DerivedLargeKBound}). A sharp reviewer will
observe that \(\mathsf{B3} \wedge \mathsf{B2}\) directly excludes cycles
in this range : combining
\(\texttt{IsOddCycle}\,n\,k \wedge k > 1322 \Rightarrow n < 2^{71}\)
with Barina's verification for \(n < 2^{71}\) yields a contradiction at
once. The Lean branch \texttt{no\_cycle\_k\_gt\_1322} is therefore an
elementary composition of \(\mathsf{B3}\) and \(\mathsf{B2}\); it
\emph{does not} add new mathematical content beyond the hypothesis. The
substantive mathematical content of the \(k > 1322\) range is entirely
inside \(\mathsf{B3}\).

\paragraph{Relationship to prior bounds.}
The \(k \leq 1322\) branch overlaps in spirit with Simons and de
Weger's~\cite{SimonsDeWeger2005} analytic small-\(k\) bound (which
ruled out cycles up to \(k \leq 68\), since extended further by the
subsequent work cited in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}). Our
contribution for \(k \leq 1322\) is therefore the full Lean
certification of the Product-Bound argument under \(\mathsf{B1}\) and
\(\mathsf{B2}\), rather than a new mathematical bound on that range.
The authors make no claim of mathematical novelty for the
\(k > 1322\) range : the role of \(\mathsf{B3}\) is to isolate the
residual analytic gap in a single, auditable Lean object ---
the formalisation of its content (via Legendre's theorem on CF
convergents, see Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}) is the advertised future
work.
